📝 Final Thoughts

Project Conclusion

Key findings, recommendations, and future directions

📌 Project Summary

What we set out to do — and what we achieved

This project applied Machine Learning to predict whether a customer will subscribe to a term deposit based on data from a Portuguese bank's telemarketing campaign. We explored the data thoroughly, preprocessed it carefully, and trained 4 classification models — comparing their performance across multiple metrics.

The project shows why model selection must reflect the objective and the cost of classification errors. The tuned Random Forest achieved the strongest positive-class F1-score in this experiment, while XGBoost achieved the highest accuracy but missed most actual subscribers at the default threshold. These are offline results, not evidence of proven campaign savings or deployment readiness.

💡 Updated Key Findings

Interpretation of the corrected pre-call prediction experiment

Evaluation setup: stratified 80:20 train/test split; 3-fold stratified cross-validation on the training set; call duration excluded because it is unavailable before a campaign call. The majority-class baseline achieves 88.30% accuracy but zero recall for subscribers.

Random Forest: strongest F1

Random Forest achieved a positive-class F1-score of 0.448 and recall of 0.606, the strongest F1 among the evaluated candidate models.

XGBoost: accuracy trade-off

XGBoost achieved 89.40% accuracy but only 0.154 recall for subscribers. Its high accuracy does not mean it identifies most potential subscribers.

Baseline matters

The majority-class baseline reaches 88.30% accuracy by predicting no subscription for everyone. Accuracy alone is therefore inadequate for this imbalanced task.

🎯 Interpretation and Practical Implications

What the experiment supports—and what it does not

Research interpretation: The results support comparing models using class-sensitive metrics rather than selecting a model by accuracy alone. The tuned Random Forest offers the best positive-class F1 (0.462) and ROC-AUC (0.801) among the reported models, while XGBoost is more conservative in predicting subscriptions at the default threshold.
  • Choose an operating threshold only after considering the relative costs of missed subscribers and unnecessary contacts.
  • Evaluate any threshold policy on validation data before assessing it once on the held-out test set.
  • Do not use call duration for pre-call targeting: it is observed only after a call has taken place.
  • Do not interpret predictive associations as causal effects or as evidence that changing a customer's characteristics will cause subscription.

⚠️ Limitations

Constraints on interpretation and generalization

  • The dataset represents a particular bank marketing campaign context and may not generalize to other banks, periods, or populations.
  • The positive class is relatively uncommon, so performance depends on the chosen threshold and metric.
  • A single held-out test set provides one estimate; performance may vary under different temporal or external validation settings.
  • Class weighting and model selection do not eliminate all possible bias or data-quality issues.
  • The experiment is predictive, not causal, and is not a substitute for a bank's compliance, fairness, or operational review.
🏆

Final Verdict

This project successfully demonstrated that Machine Learning can significantly improve the efficiency of bank marketing campaigns. The experiment suggests that model-based targeting may help prioritize outreach, but it does not measure actual call reductions, campaign savings, or conversion improvements. A controlled pilot and business-cost analysis would be required before making those claims.

Tuned Random Forest is the strongest candidate on positive-class F1 (0.462) and ROC-AUC (0.801) in this test run. The fixed Random Forest candidate records F1 0.448 and ROC-AUC 0.798. XGBoost has the highest accuracy (89.40%) but low subscriber recall (0.154). The model choice should depend on the bank's relative costs of missed subscribers and unnecessary contacts; deployment readiness has not been established.
